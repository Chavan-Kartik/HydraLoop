# Responsible AI and Safety

HydraLoop studies fraud without teaching anyone how to commit it. That constraint shapes the
whole design, not just a disclaimer at the end. This document is the single canonical safety and
responsible-AI policy for the project.

## Scope containment

HydraLoop is a synthetic, sandboxed research prototype. It does not target live payment systems,
and it does not access:

- real cardholder data or personally identifiable information;
- production payment rails or real banking APIs;
- real merchant systems or real user accounts.

## Abstraction policy

Attacks are represented only as behavioural metadata: cadence, value laddering, device reuse,
payee novelty, and resource budgets. The system never generates or stores:

- phishing messages, scam scripts, or persuasion content;
- deepfake audio or video;
- real or synthetic identity documents or images;
- credential lists, session tokens, or exploit code;
- credential-stuffing tools, malware, or operational fraud tooling;
- step-by-step attack recipes.

Every catalog scenario carries an `abstraction_level` of `behavioral_metadata_only` and a
`harm_review` note stating what is explicitly not represented. The agentic-commerce family
follows the same rule: it models the statistical footprint of an autonomous agent, not any
prompt, instruction, or integration.

## Constrained generation

The red team's output space is a validated genome with hard numeric bounds, not free-form text.
Even when a language-model strategist is enabled, its proposals are schema-validated and clamped
before execution, and rejected proposals are logged. This makes it structurally impossible for
the generator to emit an attack description outside the abstraction policy.

## Data provenance and synthetic attestation

All traffic is generated by the digital twin from declared priors. There is no real cardholder
data and no PII at any stage. Fidelity is reported honestly: the fidelity harness agrees with
the *declared* priors and internal structural validity, and the data-credibility benchmark
reports discriminator AUC and TSTR / TRTS against a shifted or external reference rather than
claiming resemblance to proprietary ground truth we do not have.

## Data policy

All generated data is synthetic. Any external dataset used only for a fidelity comparison must be
publicly available, license-checked, documented in [ASSUMPTIONS.md](ASSUMPTIONS.md), and used only
for permitted evaluation purposes.

## LLM use policy

If a language-model strategist is enabled, it only proposes schema-constrained genome parameters;
it never generates free-form attack content. Every proposal is validated, logged, and rejected if
invalid, so the generator cannot emit anything outside the abstraction policy.

## Governance and tamper-evidence

Each generation is written to an append-only, hash-chained ledger. Every entry embeds the
previous entry's digest, so any edit to run history is detectable on the next verification. The
Audit screen recomputes the chain live and reports a break if one exists. The
`run_manifest.json` records the config hash so results trace back to the exact configuration.

## Human oversight of the defence

The policy layer can abstain: it routes uncertain, high-value decisions to manual review under a
capacity budget instead of forcing an automated decline. Decisions carry SHAP reason codes and a
counterfactual so an analyst can understand and, if needed, overturn them.

## Deployment gate

HydraLoop is designed to improve defensive understanding; it is not intended to enable, instruct,
or assist real-world fraud. Any deployment in a real payment environment would require authorized
data, privacy review, security review, regulatory review, model-risk governance, human oversight,
and operational controls.

## Known limitations

We do not hide the weak spots. See [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md) for the full list,
including fidelity being relative to declared priors, weak sentinel zero-day recall,
small-data instability, and the abstraction ceiling that is a deliberate safety trade-off.
